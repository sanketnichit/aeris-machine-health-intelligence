# CWRU extension — frozen v1

The original eight recordings (105–108, 130–133) are observed and excluded. This is a new same-platform descriptive evaluation, not independent-platform replication.

Population: normal baseline 97–100 (48 kHz), ball 0.007 inch 118–121, inner race 0.014 inch 169–172, ball 0.014 inch 185–188 and centered outer race 0.014 inch 197–200 (fault recordings 12 kHz). Four load settings per group. Official tables: https://engineering.case.edu/bearingdatacenter/normal-baseline-data and https://engineering.case.edu/bearingdatacenter/12k-drive-end-bearing-fault-data. Normal baseline sampling rate follows Smith and Randall (2015), Rolling element bearing diagnostics using the Case Western Reserve University data: A benchmark study, Table A1. The rolling-element order 4.7135 is taken directly from https://engineering.case.edu/bearingdatacenter/bearing-information (not substituted by half the listed order).

Take the exact X{id:03d}_DE_time variable, including when additional variables exist. Reject missing, nonfinite or incomplete channels. For normal files only, resample the entire signal using scipy.signal.resample_poly(up=1,down=4,window=("kaiser",5.0),padtype="constant"). Effective rate 12 kHz for all. Use first six nonoverlapping complete 8192-sample windows from each file; 120 windows, 983040 effective samples. Fixed nominal speeds 1797,1772,1750,1730 rpm as in original audit.

Keep periodic Hann, normalize within each representation, fixed Butterworth 4th-order bandpass 2000–5000 Hz and Hilbert envelope; use first three harmonics of inner 5.4152, outer 3.5848 and rolling element 4.7135 orders, tolerance ±2 FFT bins per harmonic and union within each order. No optimizing bands, thresholds or speed. Compare medians per recording. Primary: expected-order fraction strictly exceeds both competing orders among 16 fault recordings, count by representation and defect category. Report ties as not correct. Secondary: expected-order fraction improvement envelope vs raw, separately by class and diameter; compare maximum of three order fractions across every normal/fault recording pair (ties half, descriptive AUC) and report all four normal maxima. This is not a calibrated classifier or decision threshold; no accuracy claim for normal state.

No training, window-random splits, tuning or p-values. Same rig and repeated conditions are correlated; the normal acquisition-rate difference may confound comparisons even after resampling. No new physical validation of RUL or field diagnostics. Preserve negative outcomes and source hashes, revision and environment. Do not change original audit or its archived results.
